Peilin Meng

Hello! I'm Peilin Meng. I am an undergraduate studying Computer Science at the University of Michigan, Ann Arbor.

I'm interested in robot learning and manipulation. When I was four, my mom taught me to fold a paper boat by showing me a few times. That was all the supervision I needed. Within a week the house was full of boats — newspaper, advertising flyers, printer paper, folded on beds, tables, and the floor — each one a little better than the last. From a handful of demonstrations, a child recovers a skill that survives changes in material and place, then sharpens it through practice alone. Robots still cannot do this. Imitation learning policies, VLA models, and WAMs have carried us a long way, and yet the remaining gap does not look to me like a pure data problem. I am interested in what else is missing: what makes a demonstration generalize rather than memorize, and how behavior cloning and reinforcement learning should be joined so that a policy keeps improving once the demonstrations run out.

2023-2025, I studied Mechanical Engineering at Shanghai Jiao Tong University as part of the Dual Degree Program with UMich, where I was a research assistant in the DIDIS lab, advised by Professor Youyi Bi.

Summer 2026, I was a research intern in the RPAD lab at Carnegie Mellon University, advised by Professor David Held.
